# Pitching Your Research: The 3-Tier Framework

Goal: To help you communicate your research value clearly and confidently across different scenarios—whether you are passing a colleague in the hallway, presenting a poster at a conference, or networking with industry partners.

As part of our academic partnership, mastering this skill is essential for maximizing the impact of your work and accelerating your professional growth.

### Tier 1: The Hook (The 1-Sentence Version)

Context: 10–15 seconds. Use this to spark curiosity and invite follow-up questions. Avoid heavy jargon.

* The Formula: To solve \[Specific Problem/Pain Point], we proposed \[Core Method/Framework], which achieved \[Most Significant Result/Value].
* Example (Urban Computing): _"To address the high input uncertainty in urban traffic decision-making, we designed a human-in-the-loop reinforcement learning model that improved scheduling efficiency by 20%."_

### Tier 2: The Elevator Pitch (The 1-Minute Version)

Context: 60 seconds. Ideal for conference coffee breaks, initial networking, or brief introductions.

* The Structure (What / So What / Now What):
  * What (The Work): Briefly state the research topic and your specific technical approach.
  * So What (The Impact): Why does this matter? What limitation in existing methods does it overcome?
  * Now What (The Next Step): What is the current status, and what kind of feedback or collaboration are you seeking?
* Example (Instructional Agents): _"I'm currently working on automating instructional design using multi-agent large language model frameworks. Existing educational platforms struggle to adapt to individual educators' pedagogical styles, requiring too much manual prompt engineering. By deploying a multi-agent system, we allow different LLMs to handle specific instructional roles, drastically reducing educator workload while preserving teaching quality. We are currently testing this across several CS courses and are looking for feedback on how to best evaluate the agents' long-term reasoning capabilities."_

### Tier 3: The Mini-Talk (The 5-Minute Version)

Context: 5 minutes. Perfect for poster sessions, structured networking meetings, or when someone asks, "Can you walk me through your paper?"

* The Structure:
  1. Background & Motivation (1 min): Set the stage. Start broad, then quickly narrow down to the specific gap your research targets.
  2. Core Challenge (1 min): Why is this gap hard to fill? Highlight the bottlenecks in current baselines or existing literature.
  3. Key Innovation (1.5 min): Explain your solution. Focus on the high-level system architecture, intuition, and design choices rather than getting bogged down in complex mathematical proofs.
  4. Crucial Results (1 min): Highlight 1-2 standout metrics or visual comparisons that definitively prove your method works.
  5. Conclusion & Future Work (0.5 min): Summarize the main takeaway and briefly mention the next immediate milestone.

### Lab Action Items & Practice

To ensure these pitches become second nature, we will integrate the following exercises into our routine:

* The Timer Challenge: During the first 10 minutes of our weekly group meetings, 1-2 students will be randomly selected to deliver their 1-minute or 5-minute pitch. A timer will be displayed, and you will be stopped exactly at the limit to build a strong sense of pacing.
* Cross-Domain Peer Review: You will pair up with lab members working on entirely different sub-fields. If your partner cannot easily follow your 1-minute pitch, it means the "knowledge curse" is too strong, and the technical jargon needs to be simplified.
* Documentation: Once your paper is accepted or uploaded to arXiv, all three pitch versions must be written down and archived in the shared project folder [here](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1fnPp_UYqbYVJ4DNEuRWjIS_JR1eS3Qs5LOeS-qTrtrM/edit?usp=sharing).
